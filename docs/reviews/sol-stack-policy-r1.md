**Verdict: revise before implementation.** Stable reservations, demand paging and exclusive per-job ownership fit the design. The commit mechanism, accounting, strict guarantee and waiting-job target need decisions first. Review: Sol, 2026-10-25; source inspection only, no builds.

1. **The cap limits region placement, not just live bytes.** Evidence: DESIGN’s “Reserve ahead”; `emit.rs:4614–4659` requests whole regions; `nl_runtime.c:277–296` checks their ends. A 5 GiB local list holding one item therefore fails immediately under a 4 GiB cap. Low RSS does not establish that a workload fits its address range. T17 reports a billion-byte region with only 8 MB touched; T23 preserves capacity-sized contracts. **Recommendation:** state this consequence explicitly; warn about individual oversized regions as well as the critical path. Define unknown/overflowed bounds, helper margin, page rounding and cap precedence; retain uncapped bounds for inspection and `stack_peak`.

2. **Windows cannot implement sparse commitment using only the top check.** Evidence: DESIGN’s Commit bullet; the allocator above advances past untouched capacity. Committing everything below that top charges unused regions; committing only near it leaves earlier headers, builder payloads and separated SoA lanes inaccessible. A builder can subsequently grow without moving the top. [VirtualAlloc](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/memoryapi/nf-memoryapi-virtualalloc) requires commitment before access. **Recommendation:** separate address allocation from ensuring writable ranges, covering initialization, append, copies and runtime I/O destinations. Track sparse committed chunks; never include the terminal guard. Batch/hoist checks where proved safe, and benchmark repeated 64 KiB commits in tight loops before promising “one compare.”

3. **Linux reservation, commitment and residency are different measures.** Evidence: `nl_context_create:168–182` already reserves `PROT_NONE | MAP_NORESERVE`, then makes the whole usable range writable. Leaving it `PROT_NONE` would fault on valid accesses. The hook at `:255` uses [mincore](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/mincore.2.html), a residency snapshot, not historical writes or commit charge. In [strict overcommit mode](https://cdn.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/mm/overcommit-accounting.html), `MAP_NORESERVE` is ignored and writable anonymous mappings incur commitment. **Recommendation:** name separate metrics and specify supported backing modes. Preserve checked allocation plus an inaccessible end page; the guard detects bugs, while checked range exhaustion follows normal failure. Ordinary demand paging cannot promise graceful recovery from host/cgroup OOM; [memory.max](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html) can trigger process killing.

4. **Trimming needs a demand metric independent of retained pages.** Evidence: DESIGN records “peak committed bytes” and trims addresses above a multiple of that number. A warm stack retaining 100 MB still has 100 MB committed during tiny jobs, so literal measurements never trigger trimming. Sparse lanes also mean page count is not an address cutoff. **Recommendation:** record each job’s demanded page/chunk ranges separately from inherited commitment; key histories by entry, with explicit worker/process scope and sample sufficiency. A small entry’s history must not imply that a frequently alternating large entry is cold. Trim only an exclusively free stack after cleanup has stopped I/O, chunks and writer access (Failures steps 2–5). Round/clamp to usable pages, retain the guard, check errors. [MADV_DONTNEED](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/madvise.2.html) discards private contents and causes later zero-fill faults; it is not Windows-style commit release. Measure first-job latency after trimming, not only warm throughput.

5. **Pooling is coherent, but ownership and compatibility need precision.** Evidence: Thread kinds correctly retains suspended frames, but still says “N data stacks”; the introduction says only the job touches its stack, while Parallel loops permits chunk reads and writes to caller-owned result slices. **Recommendation:** update to one data stack; distinguish exclusive allocation/top ownership from scoped disjoint access. Never pool suspended stacks or parents awaiting joins. Reuse only reservations large enough for the new entry, enforce its logical cap even in a larger mapping, and define size classes/eviction. Include chunk entry bounds, native stacks and writer scratch; do not relocate live reservations to grow them.

6. **`clear_stacks_between_jobs` needs a precise erasure boundary.** Evidence: pooling promises to zero what a job touched; T23’s dirty-reuse tests establish initialization behavior for covered paths, not a complete write ledger. Final tops/live lengths omit dead temporaries, failed appends and scratch; allocation peaks include untouched capacity. **Recommendation:** erase all pages that could contain that job’s writes before reuse, including failure paths and native frames if promising tenant isolation. Use a reliable dirty-range ledger or explicitly conservative erasure. Do not memset the whole reservation and destroy sparse-memory behavior.

7. **`strict_stack` overpromises fault freedom.** Evidence: both the new bullet and Decided entry promise no mid-run failure; Static allocation explicitly separates commit, prefault and locking, and says job stacks are never locked. Successful commit does not remove first-touch faults or later paging. **Recommendation:** define strict as full-bound backing with startup refusal, reject unknown bounds and cap violations, and narrow the promise to data-stack capacity/backing. Require separate prefault/locking policy for latency guarantees. Strict stacks must not be trimmed/decommitted; scheduler support must preprovision a bounded concurrency quota, since startup cannot fund unlimited future jobs. Globals retain their separate startup policy.

8. **Admission is presently a heuristic, not a memory guarantee.** Evidence: admission checks starts only, while running jobs can subsequently grow; waiting jobs and warm free stacks retain memory. **Recommendation:** count pooled, running and waiting commitment once, plus globals, snapshots, frozen batches, native stacks and overhead, under aggregate process/container limits. Specify growth-time budget enforcement and failure behavior, plus independent job-count and address-space limits. Native-stack sizing remains future work: T17 only reports bounds; T19 uses the current pthread stack. Allocation history may change resource failures/timing; it must not change successful results, parallel reduction order or replay semantics.

9. **The 10,000-job example does not establish feasibility.** Evidence: Validation requires under 1 GB even after previously busy stacks; retaining a 256 KiB floor on 10,000 reused stacks already costs about 2.44 GiB, before native frames. Five-minute idle trimming cannot touch suspended jobs. The roughly 39 TiB reservation arithmetic also excludes other mappings and platform limits. **Recommendation:** reconcile the floor/acceptance target; measure fresh and warm suspensions. [RLIMIT_AS](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrlimit.2.html) limits reservations regardless of RSS (T17 already demonstrates refusal). Qualify the 128 TiB assumption. [Fork](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/fork.2.html) copies page tables and can face additional [commit checks](https://cdn.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/mm/overcommit-accounting.html); exec does not remove that preceding risk. Prefer an appropriate spawn path and test under constrained limits.

10. **Make the first task a Linux reservation-cap change.** Evidence: `driver.rs:626` launches each test as a fresh process; emitted entries create/destroy one context; no scheduler/pool exists. **Recommendation:** add per-entry cap configuration and warnings; use the configured cap for unknown bounds and diagnose arithmetic overflow. Retain initializer scratch bounds, helper margin, guards and uncapped budget tests; rename range exhaustion. Test cap boundaries, oversized sparse regions, low-address-limit refusal and overflow. Defer pooling, histories, trimming, clearing, admission and suspension to the scheduler; implement strict backing and Windows commitment as separate tasks after the above contracts are settled. Reject unsupported options rather than silently accepting them.

# T32: faster operator matching and number output

Same rules (docs/PLAN.md). From Claude's profile of the self-hosted lexer (2026-10-25, `perf` on `lex` over the 54,145-line benchmark at -O3): `nl_ascii_format_i64_radix` 17.6%, `memmove` 18.4%, `output_token_dump` 14.1%, `nl_stack_alloc` + `nl_context_set_top` 14.6% (fixed in O1, not here), `matches_ascii` 17.3%, `lex_file` 10.9%. Release Rust spends about 60% on formatting and allocation and about 9% lexing. Two fixes here; the third (inline stack allocation) is O1's.

1. **First-byte dispatch in the self-hosted lexer** (selfhost/lexer.nl): replace the longest-first chain of up to 24 `matches_ascii` calls with a `match` on the current byte's value, then checks of the next one or two bytes for multi-byte operators (`..=`, `>>=`, `?.`, `??`, `->`, `-%` and the rest), producing exactly the same tokens, errors and positions. Keep the S1a comparisons (lex and parse, directed, mutation, benchmark) byte-identical.
2. **Fast decimal number output** (runtime and compiler): a decimal fast path for `str(n)` (and the int/long formatting behind `print`) that writes two digits at a time from a 200-byte table, sizes the output from the value's digit count, and, when the result goes straight into a builder (`out += str(n)`, `try_append(str(n))`, Bytes and Ascii and Text builders), writes the digits directly into the builder's free space instead of formatting into a temporary and copying (T15's append mode). Other radices keep the general routine. Exact output unchanged, including the minimum `int` and `long`, zero and negatives. A test comparing every boundary and a few million random values against the general routine.
3. **Measure:** best of 5, interleaved before and after, on `lex` and `parse --tokens --tree` over the benchmark, for the self-hosted lexer and parser and, for the formatting change, a small program printing ten million numbers; profile again with perf and report the new breakdown (the perf binary is /usr/lib/linux-tools/6.8.0-147-generic/perf; /usr/bin/perf is a wrapper that fails on this WSL kernel).
